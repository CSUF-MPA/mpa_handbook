Part I · Welcome

1 Introduction

PDF p. 1 of the handbook (opens in new tab)

Welcome to the Master of Public Administration (MPA) program at California State University, Fullerton (CSUF). This handbook explains the program’s requirements, policies, milestones, and student resources.

Please note

This handbook is a guide for students. The CSUF University Catalog, University Policy Statements, and official university web pages are the authoritative sources for program requirements, policies, and procedures. If this handbook differs from an official university publication, the official publication governs.

Worth knowing for 2026–27

A few rules in this year’s handbook that are easy to miss. Each links to the full text.

  • Comprehensive exam: High Pass, Pass, and Low Pass are all passing outcomes; a student who fails has one opportunity to retake the exam.
  • Advisor-approved electives: there is no fixed count. You take as many as you need to bring the Study Plan to 36 units.
  • Internship: the MPA Director or MPA Advisor decides whether POSC 497 applies to you; when it does, it counts inside the 36 units.
  • Leaves of absence: up to two consecutive semesters, requested before the first day of classes.
  • Academic standards summarize UPS 410.106, Academic Standards for Graduate Degree Students, effective June 16, 2026.
  • Generative AI: unauthorized use may constitute academic dishonesty. Follow each syllabus, and ask when the rules are unclear.

Acknowledgment of Effort

The CSUF MPA Student Handbook was originally developed by Dr. Samuel B. Stone, who served as its primary author and editor from 2015 to 2023. His work gave MPA students a structured and accessible guide to the program.

The handbook is currently maintained by Dr. David P. Adams, who continues to update and refine the content to ensure that it remains relevant and useful for current and future MPA students.

2 Mission, Vision, and Values

PDF p. 1 of the handbook (opens in new tab)

Mission

We prepare leaders to address complex social issues, uphold democratic values, and foster a commitment to ethical, equitable, and inclusive public service in Orange County and beyond.

Vision

Our vision is to be a program recognized for excellence in value-driven public service and community engagement.

Program Values

Our program values reflect the core commitments of public administration. They guide our teaching, scholarship, and community engagement, and shape the professional identity we foster in future public service leaders.

  • Accountability Promoting transparency, responsiveness, and evidence-informed decisions that uphold public trust and integrity.
  • Ethics Advancing integrity, fairness, and service to the public good by fostering ethical reasoning and critical awareness in public administration.
  • Collaboration Building skills for cross-sector partnerships, networked governance, and inclusive stakeholder engagement.
  • Lifelong Learning Instilling habits of continuous learning, reflective practice, and professional development to support adaptive leaders and long-term impact.

3 NASPAA Accreditation

PDF p. 2 of the handbook (opens in new tab)

The MPA program is accredited by the Network of Schools of Public Policy, Affairs and Administration (NASPAA). The curriculum is designed to develop the following competencies:

  1. Lead and manage in public governance
  2. Participate in and contribute to the policy process
  3. Analyze, synthesize, think critically, solve problems and make decisions
  4. Articulate and apply a public service perspective
  5. Communicate and interact productively with a diverse and changing workforce and citizenry

The program has been accredited since 1989 and was most recently reaccredited by NASPAA in 2025. To learn more, visit the NASPAA website.

4 General Facts

CSUF has offered the MPA program since 1968, making it Orange County’s original public MPA program. The program typically enrolls about 100 students and graduates approximately 30 students each year. Alumni work in local governments, county agencies, and nonprofit organizations, particularly in Orange and Los Angeles counties.

Curriculum and Format

The curriculum emphasizes ethical and evidence-based decision-making and prepares students for management and leadership roles in the public sector. Because most students are working professionals, courses are offered in the evening from 7:00 to 9:45 PM, Monday through Thursday, in face-to-face, synchronous online, and hybrid formats. Students typically take one or two courses per semester.

International students must enroll in at least 9 units during the fall and spring semesters to maintain full-time status.

Student Profile

Most students are working professionals with at least three years of experience in public agencies. Many enroll because their supervisors recommend the MPA as a step toward leadership. Others already hold supervisory or managerial roles and seek to build on their expertise. A smaller number of pre-service students enroll to gain foundational knowledge before entering the field.

Program Structure

The program does not currently offer a dual-degree option. It is supported by full-time faculty whose credentials are listed at the end of this handbook. The MPA program is housed in the Division of Politics, Administration & Justice (Gordon Hall 511), part of the College of Humanities and Social Sciences.

To be considered for admission to the MPA program, applicants must have a minimum undergraduate GPA of 2.75. The average GPA of admitted students is approximately 3.3. Because the program receives more applications than it can accommodate, admission is competitive.

Experience in the public or nonprofit sector strengthens an application. The statement of purpose allows applicants to demonstrate graduate-level writing and explain their preparation and goals. The program reviews applications holistically, considering academic performance, professional experience, the written statement, and letters of recommendation. All required materials must be submitted regardless of an applicant’s GPA.

GRE scores are not required. The program welcomes applicants from diverse academic and professional backgrounds who are committed to advancing in public service.

Part III · The Degree

8 Structure of the Program

PDF p. 8 of the handbook (opens in new tab)

The MPA degree requires 36 units, normally completed as 12 three-unit courses. Students must earn at least a 3.0 GPA in both their Study Plan and applicable graduate coursework, earn at least a C in every Study Plan course, and pass the comprehensive examination in POSC 521.

Degree at a Glance

  1. 1
    Complete the six required public administration core courses.
  2. 2
    Complete at least one breadth course: POSC 519, POSC 525, or POSC 528.
  3. 3
    Select one concentration and complete its requirements.
  4. 4
    Complete enough advisor-approved electives to bring the Study Plan to 36 units.
  5. 5
    Complete POSC 497 if the MPA Director or MPA Advisor determines that the internship requirement applies.
  6. 6
    Take POSC 521 and the comprehensive examination in the final semester, after completing all other MPA core courses.

One course, several requirements, three units

Some courses appear in both the public administration core and a concentration. A course may satisfy more than one categorical requirement, but its three units count only once toward the 36-unit degree. The number of advisor-approved electives therefore varies by concentration, course selection, transfer credit, and any internship requirement. Students typically reach out to the Graduate Advisor to inquire whether the internship requirement applies. The approved Study Plan and Titan Degree Audit (TDA) show the exact courses each student must complete.

Core Courses

Students are required to take courses in:

  • Introduction to Public Administration
  • Organizational theory
  • Research methods
  • Public finance
  • Human resources management
  • Local government management, state and local government, or public policy
  • A capstone course in public administration theory

Concentrations

The program offers concentrations in Human Resources Management, Local Government Management, Public Finance Management, and Public Policy. Every student must select exactly one concentration. The MPA degree requires 36 units regardless of the concentration selected.

Prerequisites

Students admitted to the program must have completed courses in three areas:

  1. Before your first MPA course American Government or U.S. History Must be completed before beginning MPA coursework.
  2. By the end of semester 2 Statistics or Research Methods Any course in statistics or quantitative research methods is sufficient.
  3. By the end of semester 2 Macro- or Microeconomics Any course in economics is sufficient.

Prerequisites may be completed at any undergraduate level at an accredited college or university, including a community college. Students must earn a C or better. Prerequisites do not count toward the graduate degree or graduate GPA.

The admission notice identifies any outstanding prerequisites. Students who complete a prerequisite after admission must submit a transcript documenting completion to the MPA Advisor before the concentration can be added to the Study Plan.

Public Administration Core

Students must take all of the following courses:

  • POSC 509 Foundations of Public Administration
  • POSC 521 Capstone Seminar: Public Administration Theory
  • POSC 523 Administrative Research and Analysis
  • POSC 526 Public Organizational Theory & Behavior
  • POSC 571 Public Budgeting and Finance
  • POSC 572 Human Resources Management for Public Organizations

And at least one of the following:

  • POSC 519 State and Local Government
  • POSC 525 Local Government Management
  • POSC 528 Seminar in Public Policy

All core courses must be completed before students take the comprehensive examination.

10 Internship

PDF p. 10 of the handbook (opens in new tab)

Students without sufficient recent administrative or managerial experience in the public or nonprofit sector must complete:

  • POSC 497 Internship in Public Administration

The MPA Director or MPA Advisor determines whether the internship requirement applies after reviewing the student’s professional background. Because the relevance and recency of experience vary by position, students should obtain this determination early in the program and ensure that it is reflected on the Study Plan. When required, POSC 497 is included in the 36-unit Study Plan; the MPA Director or MPA Advisor will identify how it applies to the student’s remaining requirements.

POSC 497 is a three-unit course with written assignments and 300 hours of supervised experience in a public or nonprofit organization. Consistent with the program’s mission, many students intern in local government. The program maintains placement leads developed through longstanding relationships with agencies and alumni.

Students may also complete an approved internship in Washington, D.C., through the Cal State DC program. Students interested in this option should consult the MPA Director before applying.

Historically, approximately one-third of CSUF MPA internships have been paid. Compensation and placement availability vary, and students are responsible for securing an approved position with assistance from the program.

To help offset the financial burden of an unpaid internship, Bruce Lawson (CSUF MPA 1972) has supported the Lawson Internship in Public Service Award since 2010. The award provides a $2,000 scholarship to at least one POSC 497 student each semester through a competitive application reviewed by program faculty.

12 Comprehensive Examination

PDF p. 19 of the handbook (opens in new tab)

Key information

  • The comprehensive examination is a graduation requirement that assesses mastery of core public administration concepts.
  • Students answer one general public administration question and demonstrate their ability to synthesize and apply knowledge from across the MPA curriculum.
  • Students should begin preparing early and retain notes and course materials from all core courses.
Format
Take-home, one week to complete
Question
One general public administration question
Grading
Blind grading to ensure fairness
When
In POSC 521, your final semester

Overview

The comprehensive examination is integrated into POSC 521: Capstone Seminar in Public Administration Theory, which serves as the culminating experience of the MPA program. The examination evaluates a student’s ability to:

  • Understand and apply core public administration theories and frameworks
  • Analyze and synthesize public administration literature
  • Apply theoretical concepts to real-world scenarios
  • Integrate knowledge from across the MPA curriculum

Eligibility Requirements

  • To take POSC 521 and the comprehensive examination, students must have successfully completed all core MPA courses.
  • The capstone course should be taken in the final semester after fulfilling all other core requirements.

Grading Criteria

  • Faculty readers evaluate responses independently through a blind-grading process.
  • Students receive one of the following outcomes: High Pass, Pass, Low Pass, or Fail. High Pass, Pass, and Low Pass are passing outcomes.
  • A failing outcome is assigned when at least two readers determine that the response does not meet the required standards.

Retake Policy

  1. Attempt 1

    Comprehensive examination

    Taken during POSC 521.

    High Pass, Pass, or Low Pass: requirement met

    Fail: go to the retake

  2. Attempt 2

    One retake

    A student who fails the examination has one opportunity to retake it. The retake occurs within the same semester, shortly after results are released.

    Not passing the retake makes the student subject to disqualification from the program.

  3. At faculty discretion

    Oral examination

    At their discretion, the MPA faculty may first offer the student an oral examination as a final opportunity.

    Failure to pass an offered oral examination results in disqualification from the MPA program.

Additional Details

  • Comprehensive examination guidelines, including format and evaluation criteria, will be provided during POSC 521.
  • Students are encouraged to engage with faculty and peers throughout the course to refine their understanding of core concepts.

Students with Disabilities

Students seeking examination accommodations should register with Disability Support Services (DSS) and follow the DSS accommodation process before the examination.

13 Advising

PDF p. 21 of the handbook (opens in new tab)

During the fall and spring semesters, the MPA Advisor provides primary academic advising. Assistance includes:

  • Academic program planning
  • Selecting a concentration
  • Evaluating transfer credits
  • Choosing electives
  • Understanding administrative procedures

The MPA Director can perform the same advising functions. The MPA Director provides most advising during winter and summer terms, when the MPA Advisor is normally off contract, and is also available for program-level questions throughout the year. Other faculty members are available for individual mentorship.

Advising schedules are announced each semester. Students may contact the MPA Advisor directly by email or use the program’s advising address, MPAadvising@fullerton.edu, for questions or appointments. The Division Office can be reached at 657-278-3521, Monday–Friday, 8:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m.

Students are responsible for scheduling their advising appointments and staying informed about program requirements. Advising is required at least twice:

  1. During the second semester or after approximately 12 units, to declare a concentration and confirm the Study Plan.
  2. Before the final semester, to review the TDA and prepare for graduation.

Students should retain copies of transcripts, grades, approved petitions, advising correspondence, and Study Plan changes.

Titan Degree Audit (TDA)

The Titan Degree Audit (TDA) is an official tool used to track academic progress. It helps students:

  • Monitor how completed coursework applies to degree and concentration requirements.
  • Ensure they are meeting graduation requirements.
  • Plan their remaining coursework effectively.

The TDA is available through the CSUF Student Portal. Instructions are available in the Titan Degree Audit tutorial.

Initial Study Plan

Upon admission, every MPA student is placed on an initial Study Plan that includes the following core courses:

  • POSC 509 Foundations of Public Administration
  • POSC 523 Administrative Research and Analysis
  • POSC 526 Public Organizational Theory & Behavior
  • POSC 571 Public Budgeting and Finance
  • POSC 572 Human Resources Management for Public Organizations

The initial TDA displays these courses until the student meets with the MPA Advisor or MPA Director and formally selects a concentration. Once the concentration is recorded, the remaining degree requirements appear on the TDA.

15 CSUF Graduate Academic Standards

PDF p. 24 of the handbook (opens in new tab)

This section summarizes the provisions of UPS 410.106, Academic Standards for Graduate Degree Students, effective June 16, 2026, that are most relevant to MPA students. The official policy and University Catalog govern if this summary differs from them.

The numbers at a glance

  • 3.0minimum GPA, in both graduate and Study Plan coursework
  • Cor better in every Study Plan course
  • 5 yrsto finish (10 semesters); petition for up to 7
  • 21units minimum completed in residence
  • 24units minimum carrying letter grades
  • 3400-level courses (9 units) maximum
  • 6units maximum of independent study
  • 9Study Plan units before advancing to candidacy
MPA and University Requirements

University policy requires at least 30 approved units for a master’s degree; the CSUF MPA requires 36 units. The following rules apply to the MPA Study Plan:

  • At least 21 units must be completed in residence as part of the CSUF degree program. Transfer courses and courses taken through CSUF Extension are not in-residence units.
  • Study Plan courses must be at the 400, 500, or equivalent level. A 400-level course must be approved for graduate credit; a 400-level General Education course cannot count.
  • Admission prerequisites cannot count toward the Study Plan.
  • At least 24 units must carry letter grades.
  • No course applied to another degree may be included on the Study Plan.
  • No more than six units of independent study may be included.
  • Any Study Plan change outside the approved curriculum requires the approval of the MPA Advisor or MPA Director and the appropriate Provost designee.

The MPA’s additional limit is explained in the later section on 400-level courses.

Time Limit

Students must complete all Study Plan courses within five years. This period covers 10 consecutive semesters and begins with the earliest course on the Study Plan. A student may petition to extend the limit to seven years (14 consecutive semesters).

With the required approvals, no more than nine units of outdated coursework may be validated. Coursework more than 10 years old and outdated transfer coursework cannot be validated. Students approaching the five-year limit should contact the MPA Advisor or MPA Director before the limit expires.

Continuous Enrollment

Students must remain enrolled in every fall and spring semester from admission until the degree is awarded. A student who is not taking a regular course may maintain enrollment through an approved leave of absence or, when eligible, GRAD 700:

  • GRAD 700 is available only after the student has enrolled in all Study Plan coursework, has advisor approval, and is still completing the culminating experience.
  • International students also need approval from International Student Services to enroll in GRAD 700.
  • A student who does not maintain continuous enrollment must reapply to the university and meet the admission standards in effect for the semester of readmission.
Advancement to Candidacy

Students must advance to candidacy before degree conferral. A student is eligible after attaining classified standing and completing at least nine Study Plan units. The MPA Advisor or MPA Director reviews and approves the Study Plan used for degree conferral.

MPA Culminating Experience

University policy permits each master’s program to designate an appropriate culminating experience. For the MPA, the required culminating experience is the comprehensive examination administered in POSC 521: Capstone Seminar in Public Administration Theory. The MPA does not offer a thesis or project in place of this examination.

Classified and Conditionally Classified Standing

Most MPA students are admitted in classified standing, which indicates that no additional preparatory conditions remain. An applicant who needs specific preparation for graduate study may be admitted in conditionally classified standing. The admission notice must identify the required courses, grades, and completion period. These conditions should be completed within two fall or spring semesters.

At the end of that period, the MPA Advisor or MPA Director reviews the student’s progress and recommends either classified standing or discontinuation if the conditions have not been met. An applicant may appeal an admission decision within 15 days; no more than one admission appeal may be submitted per semester.

GPA and Repeated Courses

Students must maintain at least a 3.0 GPA in both:

  • All graduate-level units attempted after admission to the degree program
  • All Study Plan coursework

Every Study Plan course must be completed with a C (2.0) or better. If a course must be repeated, it may be repeated only once; both grades remain in the GPA calculation, and the repeated course provides no additional unit credit.

With prior approval, a student may request to add or repeat up to six units to raise the Study Plan GPA to 3.0. Any substitution for a course with an unsatisfactory grade requires approval from the MPA Advisor or MPA Director and the appropriate Provost designee.

Academic Notice and Disqualification

A student is placed on academic notice if either the applicable graduate GPA or Study Plan GPA falls below 3.0. Academic notice may also result from repeated withdrawals, failure to make progress, noncompliance with an academic requirement, failure to meet the profession’s standards of competence or fitness, or conduct addressed by applicable university policy.

A master’s student on academic notice must raise both GPAs to at least 3.0 by the end of the second regular semester following the term in which a GPA first fell below 3.0. A student may be disqualified if the GPAs are not raised within that period or cannot mathematically be raised within it. Disqualification removes the student from the program and from graduate standing.

If the MPA program has no separate appeal policy, the Graduate Disqualification Appeals Committee reviews an appeal. A student disqualified from a master’s program may apply for readmission to the same program after one calendar year. Readmission requires a new Study Plan that meets current requirements; use of prior coursework requires university approval.

Prior and Transfer Coursework

With the required approvals, a master’s student may petition to apply up to nine units of 400- or 500-level coursework completed while an undergraduate at CSUF if the coursework:

  • Was not used for any requirement of the bachelor’s degree
  • Falls within the student’s five-year degree-completion period
  • Was completed with a B or better

Transfer coursework must have been completed with a B or better at a regionally accredited institution or equivalent, be acceptable toward the same graduate degree at that institution, fall within the CSUF five-year degree period, and not have been applied to another degree. Transfer or prior coursework does not apply automatically; students must request evaluation by the MPA Advisor or MPA Director.

16 Leaves of Absence

PDF p. 28 of the handbook (opens in new tab)

MPA students must maintain continuous enrollment in fall and spring until the degree is awarded. A student who needs to interrupt enrollment should consult the MPA Advisor or MPA Director and request a leave rather than simply not enrolling.

Eligibility and Procedure

After completing at least one semester in residence, a conditionally classified or classified graduate student may request a leave of absence for up to two consecutive semesters. The request must be submitted before the first day of classes for the affected term. Current forms and instructions are available from Graduate Studies.

A leave may be requested for circumstances such as:

  • Illness, disability, pregnancy, or another serious personal circumstance
  • Professional development that supports the student’s career objectives
  • Active military service
  • Severe financial hardship
  • Another documented reason approved by the university

Requests are reviewed by the appropriate university officials and may require supporting documentation.

Effect of a Leave or Break in Enrollment

Approved leave

Preserves the student’s place in the university and MPA program and preserves applicable catalog rights. A leave does not extend the time limit for completing the degree.

Unapproved break

The student must reapply to CSUF and the MPA program and meet the requirements in effect at the time of readmission.

17 A Note on 400-Level Courses

PDF p. 29 of the handbook (opens in new tab)
3

Only 400-level courses approved for graduate credit may appear on a graduate Study Plan; the University Catalog identifies those courses. The MPA permits no more than three such courses, or nine units, to count toward the degree.

In a 400-level course approved for graduate credit, the instructor must assign graduate students additional readings and substantive graduate-level work. These requirements must appear in the syllabus and may not consist only of longer versions of undergraduate assignments. Graduate credit may be revoked if a course does not meet this requirement.

Students should confirm at the beginning of the term that the instructor knows they are enrolled for graduate credit.

POSC 497 Internship in Public Administration does not count toward this limit.

18 Completion of the Degree and Graduation

PDF p. 29 of the handbook (opens in new tab)

To earn the MPA, students must:

  • Complete the approved 36-unit Study Plan
  • Maintain at least a 3.0 GPA in both applicable graduate coursework and Study Plan coursework
  • Earn at least a C in every Study Plan course
  • Pass the comprehensive examination

All university and program requirements must be satisfied.

Deadlines

Except for scheduled final examinations, all degree work must be submitted by the last day of classes for the degree to be awarded at the end of that term. Degree conferral requires completion of all university requirements and all requirements on the approved Study Plan.

19 Applying for Graduation

PDF p. 30 of the handbook (opens in new tab)

Students must apply for a graduation check and pay the graduation and diploma fee by the published deadline.

The filing deadline is published in the Registration Guide and on the Graduate Studies website. The effective graduation date is the last day of the term in which all requirements are completed.

Students who do not complete the requirements in the term originally selected must request a change to the graduation date by the applicable deadline. An additional fee may apply.

Commencement

Commencement ceremonies are held at the end of the spring semester. Students become eligible to participate after filing for a graduation check and paying the required fee. Eligibility rules depend on the student’s expected graduation term.

The College normally sends commencement information in the spring. Consult the CSUF Commencement website for current eligibility, schedules, and procedures. Caps, gowns, and hoods are available through Titan Shops.

20 Academic Integrity

PDF p. 30 of the handbook (opens in new tab)

Academic dishonesty includes cheating, unauthorized collaboration, plagiarism, fabrication or falsification, helping another person commit a violation, and attempting any of these acts. Cheating includes obtaining or attempting to obtain academic credit through dishonest, deceptive, fraudulent, or unauthorized means. Plagiarism includes presenting another person’s words, ideas, data, or creative work as one’s own without appropriate attribution.

An instructor who believes academic dishonesty has occurred must follow university procedures, which include discussing the matter with the student when circumstances permit and documenting the basis for the allegation. Academic penalties and separate student-conduct consequences may apply, subject to the student’s appeal rights. The complete procedures appear in UPS 300.021, Academic Dishonesty.

Generative AI and other tools

The unauthorized use of generative AI or another technology may constitute academic dishonesty. Permitted uses vary by course and assignment, so students must follow the instructor’s syllabus and assignment directions and ask before using a tool when the rules are unclear. Similarity-checking or other review tools may help an instructor evaluate work, but their output does not by itself establish that a violation occurred.
